Materials & Chemical Researcher • Experimental Benchwork • Machine Learning • First-Principles DFT

Hi! I’m Nafis. I am a materials and chemical researcher advancing closed-loop materials design and interfacial engineering. My scientific core is built on experimental benchwork (hands-on synthesis, spectroscopy, particle kinematics, and multifunctional polymer composite processing) integrated with atomistic modeling (first-principles DFT/MD) and physics-informed generative AI (CDVAE, directional GNNs). Rather than treating computation or lab work in isolation, I connect the two: using physical characterization (FE-SEM/EDS, FTIR-ATR, UTM mechanical testing) to ground predictive models, and deploying atomistic simulations to interrogate defects in crystalline materials, decouple catalytic scaling relations in selective CO2 reduction (CO2RR), suppress parasitic HER, and design high-performance macromolecular and energy systems.

This approach has led to first-author discovery work published in Materials Today Physics (IF: 9.3) on backward-mapping optoelectronic phenotypes to chemical genomes, along with active international research collaborations with the University of Idaho, USA and the Center for Material, Climate and Energy (CMCE). Outside research, to solve the poor research infructure problem of Bangladesh and make it accessible to all, I built an community and a platform- the Research and Analysis Institute (RAI) where I have connected and undergrad, masters students, PhD students, PostDoc stiudents, Professors, researchers and research collaborators more than 2000 from different parts of the world through emailing and social networks. During working in RAI, to solve the collaboration problem and researcher isolation globally, I took a initiative Resogram, an open network that connects 1,000+ researchers worldwide.

Founding the Research and Analysis Institute (RAI)

When I first wanted to do scientific research, I didn’t have lab facilities, funding, access to testing facilities, and no one was there to guide me. No matter who I asked, local institutions simply didn't have the tools to support student research.

Instead of giving up, I decided to figure it out on my own. I hunted down open-access materials, taught myself computational modeling and Density Functional Theory (DFT), and started reaching out to professors and PhD students around the world. They taught me an important lesson: a lack of resources can always be beaten by curiosity, grit, and collaboration.

Soon, I realized I wasn’t the only one facing these walls. Thousands of brilliant students across Bangladesh were hitting the exact same roadblocks and quietly giving up on their dreams.

I wanted to change that story. So, in 2024, I founded the Research and Analysis Institute (RAI) to bring research straight to students. Today, we’ve built a collaborative ecosystem where undergraduate students learn how to read scientific papers, write research papers, and build real computational pipelines using Machine Learning and DFT, offering accessible resources alongside professional services to support their growth.

Resogram: Transforming Global Research Collaboration

Even after building a strong training pipeline at RAI, I realized a much bigger problem was holding researchers back worldwide: people were still stuck working in complete isolation.

When you look at existing academic platforms, they are mostly built to show off finished papers, not to help people start new ones. They don’t give you a way to reach out, pitch ideas, or request collaborations, leaving countless passionate students and independent researchers stranded simply because they don't have an established supervisor to guide them or connect them to international networks.

I wanted to solve this global collaboration problem once and for all. I envisioned a world where a researcher in Bangladesh and a researcher in Africa, driven by the exact same scientific passion and research interest, could easily find each other, team up, and co-author groundbreaking work together, regardless of where they are or whether they have institutional backing.

To make that vision a reality, I built Resogram, a professional collaboration platform designed specifically for researchers and innovators.

Instead of doing research alone in silos, Resogram gives scholars an open space to showcase active ideas, match with cross-border teammates by research interest, request project collaborations, and get peer feedback. Today, it connects a growing global community of researchers, proving that when barriers come down, anyone with curiosity and drive can find their team and do meaningful science.

Instructional Workshops: 7-Day DFT Simulation Training

Through the Research and Analysis Institute (RAI) platform, I organized and instructed a 7-Day Hands-on DFT Simulation Training Program conducted live on Zoom for 56 registered students. To democratize computational modeling knowledge and support self-paced learning, the complete workshop recordings were open-sourced and published on YouTube. The curriculum guided participants from foundational solid-state modeling to electronic band-gap calculations, optical absorption analysis, and publication-standard data extraction using BIOVIA Materials Studio.

→ Access the Complete 7-Day Live Workshop Playlist on YouTube

Cluster 1: Fundamentals of Molecular & Crystal Modeling

Topics Taught: Building 3D crystal structures from scratch, CIF file importation, fractional coordinate manipulations, lattice geometry modifications, surface cleavage, and vacuum-slab construction for 2D materials.
Learner Benefit: Students master visualizer tools, symmetry operations, and supercell creation required before any electronic calculation.

Cluster 2: Electronic Structure Calculations with CASTEP & DMol3

Topics Taught: Setting up pseudopotentials, plane-wave cutoff energy convergence, k-point sampling grid selection, exchange-correlation functionals (LDA, GGA-PBE), and geometry optimization protocols.
Learner Benefit: Students learn to optimize total ground-state energy, avoid numerical artifacts, and achieve computationally efficient self-consistent field (SCF) convergence.

Cluster 3: Property Extraction & Electronic Characterization

Topics Taught: Electronic band structure generation, Total & Projected Density of States (TDOS/PDOS), Mulliken/Hirshfeld charge population analysis, and charge density difference mapping.
Learner Benefit: Learners acquire the skills to interpret orbital hybridization, determine direct vs. indirect band gaps, and evaluate covalent/ionic bonding characteristics in functional materials.

Cluster 4: Optical & Mechanical Property Prediction

Topics Taught: Computing dielectric response functions, optical absorption spectra, reflectivity, refractive index, elastic constants (Cij), and mechanical stability criteria.
Learner Benefit: Empowers participants to screen novel candidate materials directly for photovoltaic, optoelectronic, catalytic, and structural applications with publication-quality visualizations.
